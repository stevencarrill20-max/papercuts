## Camera Performance

papercutsPROB bills are designed from the ground up to photograph well. The details that make prop money look authentic to the naked eye are the same ones that translate on camera:

**Color accuracy** — Our printing process reproduces the characteristic green tones and intricate color gradients of US currency with precision. Bills don't go flat or shift hue under studio lighting or natural light.

**Sharp print detail** — Fine linework, portrait detail, and serial number typography are crisp at close focus distances, meaning your macro and close-up shots hold up to full-resolution scrutiny.

**Minimal glare** — The bill stock and finish are selected to reduce the harsh reflections that can blow out highlights and flatten the texture of currency in photographs. You get dimension and depth, not a washed-out surface.

**Consistent stacking and fanning** — Our bills handle the way real currency does, so they fan, stack, fold, and crumple naturally in styled shots without looking stiff or artificial.

## Shoot Tips

Getting the most out of your prop money on a photography shoot comes down to a few practical techniques:

- **Use quality lighting** — Soft, directional light brings out the texture and detail in prop bills. Avoid flat overhead lighting that flattens the surface and loses the dimensionality of the print.
- **Mix denominations for visual depth** — Combining different bill denominations adds realism and visual interest. A stack of identical bills reads as theatrical; a mixed stack reads as authentic.
- **Fan bills for the classic spread** — The fanned-out money spread is an iconic image for a reason. Slightly overlap bills at irregular angles for a natural look, and use a small amount of wax adhesive or styling tape behind the scenes to hold the arrangement without damaging the bills.
- **Add coins for texture contrast** — Coins scattered or stacked alongside bills create contrast in texture, material, and color that makes styled compositions more dynamic and visually engaging.
- **Shoot tethered when possible** — For commercial clients, shooting tethered lets you verify that print detail is resolving correctly at your intended output size before you wrap the shoot.
