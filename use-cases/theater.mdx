## Stage-Specific Considerations

Theater demands a different set of priorities from prop money than film or photography. When selecting and preparing prop bills for a stage production, keep these factors in mind:

**Visibility under stage lighting** — Stage lighting environments vary enormously, from warm tungsten washes to cool LED rigs to high-intensity spotlights. papercutsPROB bills maintain their visual legibility and color contrast across a wide range of lighting conditions, ensuring the audience always reads "money" instantly and clearly.

**Readability at distance** — In a live performance, the audience can't lean in for a closer look. Larger denomination bills — with their bold numeral typography and high-contrast design — are your best tool for communicating value from a distance. A $100 bill reads as significant money; a $1 bill requires explanation.

**Consistency across performances** — Your prop department needs confidence that the bills used in Tuesday's matinee look the same as those used in Saturday's evening show. Our consistent print quality ensures your prop money stays reliable across your entire run.

## Longevity Tips

Making your prop money last through a full production run is a matter of consistent, disciplined handling and storage. Follow these practices to protect your investment:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Store Flat and Dry">
    Store prop bills flat in a labeled folder, envelope, or binder between performances. Avoid folding or crumpling bills for storage — repeated creasing accelerates wear. Keep storage away from moisture, which can cause bills to warp or stick together.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rotate Your Stock">
    If your budget allows, purchase two sets of prop money and rotate between them across the run. Rotating reduces the per-bill handling frequency and extends the life of both sets significantly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Handle with Clean, Dry Hands">
    Brief your cast and stage crew to handle prop money with clean, dry hands. Oils and moisture from skin are the primary causes of surface degradation on paper currency — real or prop.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect Before Each Performance">
    Build a quick prop inspection into your pre-show checklist. Replace any bills that have become excessively worn, torn, or visually degraded before they hit the stage — a damaged bill reads as damaged, not as money.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post-Show Collection">
    Collect all prop money immediately after each performance and return it to secure storage. Don't leave bills on set overnight or in dressing rooms where they may be mishandled or go missing.
  </Step>
</Steps>
