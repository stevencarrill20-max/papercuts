## Why Prop Money Matters on Set

A scene built around money is only as convincing as the bills in the actor's hands. Poorly printed or obviously fake prop currency pulls viewers out of the story the moment it appears on screen. Realistic prop money, by contrast, reinforces the world you're building — it signals to the audience that the stakes are real, the characters are credible, and the production is professional. papercutsPROB bills are printed with sharp detail, accurate color grading, and textures designed to read authentically whether you're shooting wide or going in for a tight close-up.

## On-Set Best Practices

Keeping your production safe and compliant when using prop money is just as important as making it look good on camera. Follow these guidelines on every shoot day:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Assign Prop Master Accountability">
    Designate a single prop master or prop coordinator responsible for all prop money on set. That person should maintain a written log of quantities distributed and collected at the start and end of every shooting day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Secure Storage Between Takes">
    When prop money isn't actively in use, store it in a locked prop safe, case, or container clearly labeled "PROP MONEY." Never leave bills loose or unattended on set.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep Compliance Markings in Context">
    papercutsPROB bills carry "NOT LEGAL TENDER" markings as required by federal prop money guidelines. During shooting, ensure these markings remain present on the physical props — don't alter or obscure them. If a shot requires the markings to be out of frame, that's a framing and editing decision, not a reason to remove them from the bills themselves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Control Access">
    Limit who handles prop money to cast and crew with a direct need. Brief background performers on proper handling and make sure all bills are collected before talent is released at the end of each day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post-Wrap Reconciliation">
    After your final shooting day, count all prop money against your original inventory log. Destroy or securely dispose of any bills that are too damaged to reuse, and store the remainder safely for future productions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Always order more prop money than you think you need. Action sequences, multiple takes, and spontaneous creative decisions can burn through your supply quickly — having extra on hand means your production never has to stop for a resupply run.
</Tip>
