Following industry best practices for prop money handling protects your production, your crew, and your organization — and ensures that prop money is used responsibly from the first shooting day to the final wrap. The guidelines on this page reflect standards used by professional prop departments, production companies, and educational institutions across the creative industry. Whether you're running a major film production or a classroom financial literacy program, these practices apply.

## On-Set Accountability

Accountability starts with assigning clear ownership. Every production or organization using prop money should designate a single responsible individual to oversee all prop money from acquisition to disposal.

**The assigned prop master or coordinator should:**

- Maintain a written inventory log listing the exact quantity and denominations of prop money received
- Check out and check in prop money to cast, crew, and participants at the beginning and end of each day or session
- Document any prop money that is damaged, destroyed, or otherwise removed from the active inventory during production
- Reconcile the final count against the original inventory at the conclusion of each production day and at final wrap
- Report any discrepancy between checked-out and returned quantities immediately to the production manager or department head

Treating prop money with the same accountability you'd apply to real currency on set is the single most effective way to prevent incidents.

## Storage

How you store prop money when it's not in active use is as important as how you handle it during production.

- Store all prop money in a **locked prop safe, hard case, or secured container** when not in active use on set or in session
- Label the container clearly and visibly with **"PROP MONEY — NOT LEGAL TENDER"** on the exterior
- Keep the storage container in a **controlled-access area** — not in a general holding area, green room, or public-facing space
- Do not leave prop money loose in bags, pockets, or open trays where it can be accidentally mixed with personal belongings or real currency
- In multi-day productions, store prop money on-location rather than transporting it unnecessarily, which reduces the risk of loss or misplacement in transit

## Handling

Day-to-day handling discipline is what separates a professional prop operation from one that creates problems.

- **Never mix prop money with real currency** — Keep prop bills and coins in dedicated, clearly labeled bags or envelopes at all times. Even accidental mixing creates confusion and potential legal exposure.
- Brief every cast member, background performer, and crew member who will handle prop money before they do so — even if the briefing is brief. Everyone on set should know what they're holding and what they cannot do with it.
- Do not allow cast or crew to pocket prop money at the end of a shooting day, even as a memento. All bills and coins must be returned to the prop master.
- Treat damaged or excessively worn bills as inventory items to be tracked and documented, not as waste to be casually discarded.

## Shooting with Prop Money

On-camera use of prop money requires specific attention to compliance and crew awareness.

- **Verify compliance markings before every shooting day.** Confirm that "NOT LEGAL TENDER" markings are present and legible on all prop bills being used on set.
- **Do not obscure compliance markings in a way that makes props physically indistinguishable from real currency.** If a specific shot requires the markings to be out of frame, that is a camera framing decision — not a reason to alter, tape over, or deface the markings on the physical bills.
- Inform your camera department and director of photography that prop money is in use so they can frame and light accordingly, maintaining the visual intent while preserving compliance.
- If your production requires a truly "seamless" on-camera look, consult with your legal and production team about the specific federal guidelines that govern prop money in motion picture use before modifying any bills.

## Disposal

What happens to prop money after your production wraps is just as important as what happens during it.

- **Never leave prop money unattended in a public space** — on a table, in a parking lot, on public transit, or anywhere a member of the public could pick it up and mistake it for real currency.
- At the conclusion of a production or program, collect all prop money and either:
  - **Secure it for future use** in locked, labeled storage if the production anticipates needing it again
  - **Destroy it** by shredding or cutting through the bills before disposing of them, so the remnants cannot be recovered and used deceptively
- Document the destruction of prop money in your inventory log, including the date, quantity, and method of disposal.
- If prop money has been distributed to participants in an educational or training context, collect all materials at the conclusion of the session and account for every unit before releasing participants.

<Warning>
  Allowing prop money to leave a controlled production, educational, or organizational environment irresponsibly — whether through negligent storage, unauthorized souveniring, or failure to collect materials after a session — can create serious legal and public safety issues. If prop money enters general circulation and is used in a transaction, the consequences can extend to your production company or organization. Responsible disposal and accountability are not optional.
</Warning>
